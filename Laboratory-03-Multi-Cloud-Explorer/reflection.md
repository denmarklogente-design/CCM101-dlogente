# Reflection

This mission gave me a clearer idea of how different cloud platforms can provide similar services while still having their own advantages. I learned that AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud are not exactly the same, so choosing a platform should depend on what an organization actually needs.

While comparing the three providers, I noticed that they all have services for computing, storage, networking, and identity management. However, their services are designed and presented differently. This made me realize that knowing the purpose of a service is more important than simply memorizing its name.

The client recommendation activity was also useful because each organization had different requirements. A startup may need a platform that can grow with its application, while a university may benefit from strong Microsoft compatibility. On the other hand, a company working with AI and machine learning may need cloud services that are better suited for those types of projects.

One important lesson I learned is that there is no cloud provider that can automatically be considered the best for every situation. The decision should consider factors such as the organization's existing systems, expected workload, budget, technical requirements, and future plans.

I also improved my technical documentation skills during this mission. I practiced creating comparison tables, organizing research information, recording screenshots, and writing Markdown files for my GitHub portfolio.

Overall, Mission 3 helped me understand cloud platforms from a practical point of view. Instead of only learning what each provider offers, I learned how to compare their services and choose a suitable platform based on a client's situation.
